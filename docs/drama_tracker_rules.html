Drama Tracker

1. Core Concept

A Drama Scene is a scene where the party can approach and work several NPCs at once, in any order — a masquerade ball, a noble's dinner party, a den of rival crime bosses. Unlike a single one-on-one negotiation, there's no fixed "target": on any given turn a PC can walk up to whichever NPC they like, and the next round they might try someone else entirely.

It runs on top of the existing Motive Drivers system — every NPC's wants, fears, and secrets are the same data already tracked on their sheet. This tool doesn't add new NPC data; it gives the GM a live console for using that data mid-scene: revealing motives, computing the DC swing they cause, rolling the NPC's own impulses, and tracking how each relationship is trending.

  • Every NPC tracks their own outcome independently. Winning over the countess doesn't affect your standing with the baron standing next to her.
  • No roll buttons. Dice are still rolled at the table like normal — the tracker's job is to tell you the DC and record what happened, not to roll for you.
  • History and Insight checks are your reconnaissance. They don't win the conflict on their own — they tell you which of an NPC's buttons are worth pushing before you commit to a Persuasion, Deception, or Intimidation check.

2. How a Round Plays Out

PCs act in initiative order, same as any encounter — but "acting" means picking a target and a tactic, not moving or attacking.

Step 1 Approach

On their turn, a PC picks any NPC present in the scene to talk to — doesn't have to be who they talked to last round.

Step 2 Read the Room (optional)

Attempt a History or Insight check to reveal one of that NPC's hidden motives before committing to an approach.

Step 3 Make the Play

Roleplay the approach, then roll a social check — against a revealed motive for a DC modifier, or in blind for the flat DC.

Step 4 Record It

GM logs the roll as a success or failure against that NPC's own tally. Three of either ends their arc.

3. Discovery Checks

Every NPC's motives start Hidden. A successful discovery check moves one motive up a tier — Hidden → Partial (the party learns the topic, not the strength) → Public (they learn the exact value too). The check you roll depends on how big a secret it actually is:

Absolute Value (|Value|) Discovery Tier Check Player Visibility
15 to 20 Famous / Infamous None needed — already public knowledge. Public (Auto-revealed)
10 to 14 Local Rumors History, DC 12 Gated by GM
0 to 9 Private Drive Insight, DC = 20 − |Value| Gated by GM

A motive at ±15 or beyond never needs a discovery check — it's already common knowledge, and shows up on every player's view automatically. A failed check simply finds nothing new; there's no penalty for trying.

4. Social Checks & the DC Modifier

Once a motive is revealed — Partial or Public — a PC can consciously work it, via the GM's Persuade button on that motive. Its strength determines the modifier: weak motives only nudge the difficulty, but strong motives swing Advantage or Disadvantage instead of the DC, so no single motive can make a check trivial or impossible on its own.

  • |Value| 10–20: DC stays at the standard 12. Positive (a Want) grants Advantage; negative (a Fear) imposes Disadvantage.
  • |Value| 1–9: small nudge off the standard DC — −2 for positive, +2 for negative. Rolled normally.
  • Value 0, or nothing revealed to target: the check just uses the standard DC 12, rolled normally.

DC 12 is the suggested starting point the Persuade dialog pre-fills, not a hard rule — the GM can still type over it for an unusually easy or difficult ask.

5. Resolution: Success & Failure

Every NPC in the scene keeps their own running tally, completely independent of everyone else present:

Success
Failure
  • 3 Successes — Won Over. The NPC's attitude shifts in the party's favor; the GM decides what that unlocks (an introduction, a favor, information, a deal).
  • 3 Failures — Alienated. The NPC shuts down or turns hostile toward further approaches for the rest of the scene.
  • Everyone else keeps going. One NPC resolving — either way — doesn't end the scene. The ball goes on until the GM calls it.

6. Running It at the Table

Starting a Scene

Press Shift + S as the GM. If nothing's running, this opens the setup dialog:

  • Name the scene ("The Duskmere Masquerade").
  • Mark each token on the canvas as a PC, an NPC, or leave it out.
  • Save as Preset to reuse the same cast and scene name later — presets reference the actors themselves, so they still work even if the tokens they were saved from are long gone.
  • Start Drama Scene rolls initiative for the PCs and opens the GM Panel.

The GM Panel

Pressing Shift + S again while a scene is active reopens this instead of the setup dialog. Per NPC, it gives you:

  • Their motive list, with reveal state and value at a glance.
  • A click-to-cycle reveal badge per motive (Hidden → Partial → Public → Hidden) — purely a visibility toggle, no roll attached.
  • Reveal Check (one per NPC) — pick any skill, a DC (with Easy/Average/Hard quick-set), and a roll mode, and send it to whoever currently has the turn. The GM decides what to reveal afterward, based on the result.
  • Persuade (one per visible motive) — same send-a-check flow, but pre-fills a suggested DC/roll mode from that motive's strength (see Section 4) and lets the GM pick the skill each time.
  • Success/Failure +/− buttons for that NPC's tally.
  • Add NPC for guests who wander in mid-scene, and a remove button for each existing one.

The Player HUD

Auto-opens for anyone with a PC in the scene. Shows the NPC roster, whose turn it is, and only whatever's currently been revealed — a locked icon in place of the value for anything still Partial, and nothing at all for anything still Hidden. When the GM sends a Reveal Check or Persuade request to a PC, a clearly labeled Roll <Skill> (DC X, mode) button appears right in their HUD — clicking it opens a real, interactive roll on their own client, using their own actor's modifiers.
