Motive Drivers

1. Core Concept & Scale

NPCs are driven by dynamic, list-based motivators. Each driver has a name and a value within the following scale:

$$\text{Motive Value Range: } [-20, +20]$$
  • Attraction (Positive Values, +1 to +20): Things the NPC actively desires, seeks out, or responds well to (Wants, Needs, Desires).
  • Aversion (Negative Values, -1 to -20): Things the NPC fears, avoids, or is repulsed by (Fears, Threats, Repulsions).
  • Apathy (Value of 0): Indifference toward the drive.

2. Discovery & Visibility Tiers

Motives are initially hidden from players and must be discovered through research or observation:

Absolute Value (|Value|) Discovery Tier Mechanic Player Visibility
15 to 20 Famous / Infamous Publicly known. No check needed. Public (Auto-revealed)
10 to 14 Local Rumors Whisper-tier. Revealed with a DC 12 Int (History) check. Gated by GM
0 to 9 Private Drive Deeply personal. Revealed with a Wis (Insight) check against 20 - |Value|. Gated by GM
  • Hidden: Invisible to players.
  • Partial: Players see the motive's name but the numerical value is replaced by a padlock icon (they know the subject but not its strength or direction).
  • Public: Both the name and value are visible.
  • Fame Override: Any motive with a value of ≥ 15 or ≤ -15 automatically overrides hidden/partial states, becoming visible to players.

3. Social Interaction (Persuading a Motive)

When players target a discovered motive in their roleplay, its strength determines how the check is modified - weak motives only nudge the difficulty, but strong motives swing Advantage or Disadvantage instead of the DC. This keeps any single motive from making a check trivially easy or effectively impossible on its own (a straight DC subtraction could do both, since motive values range far wider than typical DCs).

Absolute Value (|Value|) Effect
10 to 20 DC stays at the standard 12. Positive (Attraction) grants Advantage; negative (Aversion) imposes Disadvantage.
1 to 9 Small nudge off the standard DC: −2 for positive (Attraction), +2 for negative (Aversion). Rolled normally.
0 No effect - standard DC 12, rolled normally.
  • Appealing to Attraction (Positive Value): Makes the task easier - Advantage at strong values, a small DC discount at weak ones.
  • Threatening Aversion (Negative Value): Makes the task harder - Disadvantage at strong values, a small DC penalty at weak ones.
  • DC 12 is a starting point, not a hard rule - the GM can still adjust it up or down for an unusually easy or difficult ask.

4. NPC Autonomy (Temptation Saves)

When an NPC must make a choice on their own without a player active roll (e.g., deciding to flee, resisting temptation), the GM rolls a Temptation Save:

$$\text{Temptation Roll (d20)} \le \text{Absolute Motive Value}$$
  • Success (Roll ≤ Value): The NPC succumbs to their urge/fear and acts accordingly.
  • Failure (Roll > Value): The NPC maintains discipline and resists the drive.
  • A value of 20 is an automatic fail to resist; a value of 0 means they never act on it.

Advantage & Disadvantage (Roll-Under Mechanics):

Because Temptation Saves succeed on rolling low, Advantage and Disadvantage formulas are mathematically inverted to preserve the statistical meaning:

  • Advantage (Green d20 Icon): Rolls 2d20kl (Keep Lowest). Keeping the lowest result increases the likelihood of rolling under the target, making the NPC more likely to succumb to the temptation.
  • Disadvantage (Red d20 Icon): Rolls 2d20kh (Keep Highest). Keeping the highest result decreases the likelihood of rolling under, making the NPC more likely to resist the temptation.

5. Token Resource Bars

Public or famous motives can be tracked directly on token resource bars on the canvas:

  • Display: Drawn as a visual progress bar indicating intensity (0 to 20).
  • Want (Positive): Renders as a Green progress bar.
  • Aversion (Negative): Renders as a Red progress bar.
  • Interactivity: Dragging or editing the value on the token bar directly updates the motive value on the NPC sheet.
